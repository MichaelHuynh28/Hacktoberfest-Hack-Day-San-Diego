What is Star Scout?

A map of real stars that you can explore, with a friendly AI guide that answers your questions. Made for curious kids, parents, and teachers.

What you're looking at

  • Real stars. Every dot is a real star from the HYG star catalog, a big free list that astronomers made from telescope surveys. We show the roughly 9,000 stars you could see with just your eyes on a very dark night.
  • Real colors and brightness. Bigger, brighter dots are brighter in our sky. Blue-white stars are hot, and orange and red stars are cooler.
  • The Milky Way. The soft glowing band is our home galaxy, seen from inside. It holds billions of stars that are too faint to see one by one.
  • Constellations. The lines join stars into the 88 official star patterns, like Orion the Hunter.

Two ways to view the sky

Dome shows the sky the way it looks from Earth, like lying on your back and looking up. Drag to look around, and scroll to zoom.

Panorama unrolls the whole sky into one flat picture, the way a world map flattens the globe. The Milky Way runs straight across the middle, so you can see our whole galaxy at once.

Our Sun is on the map too, shown in its real position in the sky today as a big glowing yellow dot. The dashed line is the ecliptic, the path the Sun follows across the stars over a year. Click the Sun to find out how far away it is.

How to use it

  • HoverMove your mouse over a star to see its name and constellation.
  • ClickClick a star to open its fact card. Gemma writes a short explanation about it.
  • Ask GemmaType any question about that star, like “Could I live there?” or “Why is it red?”
  • Gemma TourGemma picks 5 interesting stars and flies you to each one.
  • QuestsFollow a set route of stars, such as “Find the North Star”, and finish the challenges.
  • Star LookupDon't want the map? Search for any star by name and read about it.

Kid mode (on by default) asks Gemma to use short sentences, fun comparisons, and a “Wow fact”. Turn it off for more detailed answers.

How the AI works

The explanations come from Gemma 4, an open-weight AI model made by Google. Open-weight means anyone can download the model and run it or study it. This app uses Gemma 4 through the Gemini API.

When you click a star, we send Gemma that star's real catalog numbers: how far away it is, how bright it is, its color, and its star type. We ask Gemma to stick to those facts and not make up exact numbers.

AI can still make mistakes. If something sounds surprising, check it with a grown-up, a book, or a site like NASA's. That's what real scientists do too!

For parents and teachers

  • Good for ages 8–12 with kid mode on, and for anyone curious with it off.
  • Classroom ideas: compare a red supergiant (Betelgeuse) with a blue one (Rigel). Work out when the starlight you see left its star. Find the North Star (Polaris).
  • There are no accounts and no ads. Questions go only to the AI model to get an answer.
  • Distances are in light-years, the distance light travels in one year, which is about 9.5 trillion km.
